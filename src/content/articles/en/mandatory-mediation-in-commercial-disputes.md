Since 1 January 2019, applying to a mediator before filing a lawsuit has been mandatory for commercial receivables and compensation claims for a sum of money (Turkish Commercial Code, Art. 5/A). A lawsuit filed without first applying to a mediator is dismissed on procedural grounds.

## Which disputes are covered?

The rule covers commercial claims for the payment of money. The most common in practice are:

- Invoice receivables arising from sales, service and supply contracts
- Current account balances
- Compensation claimed for breach of contract

## Where is the application made?

The application is made to the mediation office at the courthouse where the competent court is located. The office appoints a mediator from its list, who then invites the parties to a first meeting.

## How long does it take?

The mediator concludes the process within **six weeks** of appointment. Where necessary, this may be extended by up to **two weeks**. From the date of application until the final report, limitation periods are suspended and time limits for the loss of rights do not run.

## What companies should keep in mind

**Attending the first meeting.** A party who fails to attend the first meeting without a valid excuse bears all litigation costs in a later lawsuit, even if they win the case.

**An authorised representative.** For the process to produce a result, the company should be represented by someone with decision-making authority or by an authorised lawyer.

**Documents.** Having contracts, invoices, correspondence and account statements ready from the start allows the negotiation to proceed on concrete information.

## If an agreement is reached

A settlement agreement signed jointly by the parties, their lawyers and the mediator is treated as an enforceable court-judgment equivalent, without the need for an enforceability annotation. The parties cannot later sue over matters covered by the agreement.

## If no agreement is reached

The mediator draws up a final report. The original, or a copy certified by the mediator, must be attached to the statement of claim.
