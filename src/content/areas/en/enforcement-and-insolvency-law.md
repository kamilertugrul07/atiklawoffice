faqs:
  - q: "I received a payment order. How much time do I have?"
    a: "In ordinary enforcement by attachment, an objection must be filed within 7 days of service of the payment order. In proceedings based on negotiable instruments the period is 5 days. If the deadline is missed, the proceedings become final."
  - q: "Do the proceedings stop if the debtor objects?"
    a: "Yes, a timely objection stops enforcement without judgment. The creditor may continue by applying for removal of the objection within 6 months, or by filing an action for annulment of the objection within 1 year of its notification."
  - q: "Can a mediation settlement be enforced?"
    a: "Yes. A settlement agreement signed by the parties, their lawyers and the mediator has the force of a judgment and can be enforced directly. In other cases an annotation of enforceability is obtained from the court."
  - q: "I do not think I owe the debt. What should I do?"
    a: "Objecting to the payment order in time is the first step. If the proceedings have become final or the debt has been paid, a negative declaratory action or a restitution action can be brought."

In enforcement law, everything depends on time limits. If no objection is made to a payment order within seven days, the proceedings become final; the creditor has one year to seek annulment of an objection. Calculating time limits correctly determines the outcome for creditors and debtors alike.

We help creditors choose the most suitable type of proceedings based on the documents and follow the file through to collection. We also represent debtors who have received a payment order in objection and court proceedings.
